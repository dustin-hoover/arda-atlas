# Earth analogues for Middle-earth's ground

The imagery of Arda is drawn by the engine (`src/gen.js`) from our own geography. To make it read like real
satellite imagery rather than a painted map, each kind of ground is textured with small patches of real imagery cut
from an Earth landscape that resembles it in form, climate and latitude. New Zealand is the first choice; Iceland
and the Island of Hawaiʻi supply the volcanic ground of Mordor; elsewhere on Earth fills the gaps New Zealand cannot
(sand seas, steppe, tundra, taiga, sea ice).

Only texture and colour are taken from these places. No coastline, range, river or other shape of Earth is imported,
nothing is taken from any film, and no Google, Bing, Apple, Esri or Mapbox imagery is used.

Generated by `tools/analogues/textures.py` (readers in `tools/analogues/earthdata.py`) into `src/textures.js`:
for each class a fine patch (128 px at 10 or 30 m/px) and a coarse one (128 px at 80 to 250 m/px), high-passed so the
engine's own climate and terrain decide where the class lies, made tileable, and turned so that their baked sunlight
falls from the north-west like the engine's hillshade. The renderer repeats them in world coordinates with random
per-cell offsets (no visible repeat) and fades their detail out where a patch would be only a few dozen pixels wide.
The mountain ranges' colour tints from NASA Blue Marble are listed in `docs/RANGES.md`.

## Why New Zealand

The owner asked for New Zealand first: its landscapes span most of what Middle-earth needs within one country (green
pasture, tussock high country, beech and podocarp forest, glaciated alps, braided rivers, raised bogs, a volcanic
desert), and LINZ publishes the whole country at 10 m under an open licence. The places were chosen as landscape
analogues for the ground described in the books, not for any other reason.
